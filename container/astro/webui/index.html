Day-to-night exposure ramp. This doesn't shoot on its own — it uploads a target-brightness curve the head follows while a Timelapse or Path-lapse runs, ramping exposure from daylight into night. The head meters the light with the external Optical Matrix Sensor Module accessory; without it, it can't ramp. The exposure-limit and priority encodings are inferred, so the exact commands are shown for you to read before they're sent.

    Align

    The head needs to know which way is north before it can point at a star. Three ways, best first:

    Solve the sky — most accurate, no compass

    Take a frame of the night sky and let the solver work out exactly where the head is pointing, then write that as the alignment. This is the dream path — it needs no compass and no phone sensors.

    Phone compass — if this browser is on a phone

    Hold the phone flat against the head, pointing the same way the camera does, and read its magnetometer. Only works on a phone, and a magnetometer is never as good as a solve.

    Track

    Half rate splits the drift between sky and ground so each trails half as much — the landscape-astro compromise. Tracking needs the Astro Kit fitted and the head aligned; without the kit it can point and align but not track.

    Wi-Fi

    The head keeps its own access point and also joins your network, so it is reachable without switching Wi-Fi. Both radios share one channel — joining forces the AP onto your network's channel.

    Sixty seconds after the last client disconnects the head powers its radio down and the network disappears. Holding the control link open prevents that, at the cost of battery — and, while the mount is not aligned, of making the Benro app ask for a compass calibration.
